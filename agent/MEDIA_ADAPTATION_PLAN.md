# Solumora Media Adaptation Plan

## Purpose

This document turns the current Solumora canon into a practical roadmap for producing:

- one flagship video game
- one flagship animated adaptation
- a smaller animation path if a full anime production is not the first viable move

The recommendation is based on current canon structure, not on generic franchise assumptions.

## Canon Understanding Summary

Solumora is already organized around adaptation-ready pressure lines:

1. Geography is plot, not background.
   Desert Zakros is a hard equatorial barrier that shapes politics, trade, military limits, and survival culture. Terravelle and Auralis are not just different places; they are different civilizational answers to the same magical world.

2. Magic has legible rules and cost.
   Flux is universal but capacity-limited. Control Tier, sigils, Hypertext, Hand Signs, and Watts give the setting a consistent rule language that can support animation spectacle and game mechanics without hand-waving.

3. The world runs on institutions under strain.
   The strongest material in the vault is not only battles or mysteries. It is operators, clerks, scholars, guides, rulers, and logistics workers trying to prevent systems from breaking. That is the world’s differentiator.

4. The setting has a built-in flagship twist.
   The High Demons are hidden, embedded, and politically catastrophic if revealed. Verath's intervention threshold gives the setting an escalation engine that can power multiple seasons or games.

5. There is an existing central adaptation arc.
   The Kellvost Incursion, the mobilization window, the rift-cascade threat, the assembly of Merra Veld, Jess Maren, Kess Tarren, and Verath, and the public revelation sequence already form a coherent marquee story.

## What Should Be Adapted First

Do not try to adapt the entire vault first.

Do adapt the war-threshold and revelation arc first.

The best flagship adaptation spine is:

- Kellvost Incursion
- border mobilization and institutional failure
- investigation and coalition assembly
- Verath revelation
- joint stabilization operation
- ceasefire and altered world order

This uses the strongest existing canon advantages:

- political thriller structure
- expedition and mystery texture
- clear emotional stakes
- a manageable protagonist cluster
- strong season ending points
- franchise-expanding aftermath material already documented

## Flagship Animation Recommendation

### Format

Primary recommendation: serialized animated drama, 24 episodes across 2 cours.

Fallback recommendation: 8-10 episode prestige animated season.

If neither is currently financeable: produce a 22-30 minute proof-of-concept pilot plus 3 short anthology pieces.

### Why Animation Fits Solumora

- Flux visuals, rift geometry, sigil logic, and desert anomalies benefit from controlled stylization.
- The setting depends on environmental contrast: Terravelle's practical horizontality, Auralis's vertical prestige, Wolfpoint's severe cold geometry, and Zakros's lethal exposure.
- High Demon concealment and reveal work better when visual language is intentional rather than photoreal.

### Season Structure

Season 1 should cover the pre-revelation crisis arc.

Suggested structure:

1. Episodes 1-3: Kellvost aftermath, political accusation, current pressure map.
2. Episodes 4-7: Merra, Jess, and Kess on separate tracks discovering the same failure pattern.
3. Episodes 8-11: Verath enters through indirect contact, evidence chain tightens, institutions stall.
4. Episodes 12-16: coalition formation, Ashford pressure, border hardening, failed closed-door solutions.
5. Episodes 17-20: revelation threshold, Fifth Bell declaration, standstill conditions.
6. Episodes 21-24: joint anchor operation, ceasefire framework, changed world baseline.

### Core Cast for Animation

- Merra Veld: field competence, survival logic, desert realism
- Jess Maren: logistics and institutional truth-telling
- Kess Tarren: ruins scholarship and cascade diagnosis
- Teren Voss / Verath: hidden existential layer and reveal engine
- Cassia: constrained sovereign caught between legitimacy and capture
- Drest: strategic antagonist with real institutional logic
- Eddan Voss: counterweight grounded in restraint and security

### Animation Visual Bible Priorities

- Flux behavior grammar by casting mode
- sigil and Hypertext legibility rules
- kingdom architecture language guide
- clothing by class, tier, and institution
- rift-event staging rules
- High Demon concealment and reveal design language

### Animation Risks

- Too much exposition if the rules are explained instead of dramatized
- Too many named institutions too early
- starting from deep history instead of present crisis

### Animation Rule

Keep Season 1 focused on a small set of operators moving through a world larger than them. The audience should feel that the world is massive, but the story should stay navigable.

## Video Game Recommendation

### Do Not Build First

- not an MMO
- not a giant open-world RPG on first attempt
- not a pure character-action game

Those approaches would either flatten the setting into combat spectacle or demand production scale before the world has adaptation grammar.

### Recommended First Game

Build a party-based narrative RPG with tactical missions and a logistics layer.

Working concept:

- story-driven, mission-structured RPG
- squad of 3-5 key operators
- tactical encounters around rifts, expeditions, checkpoints, extraction, and stabilizing infrastructure
- between-mission planning around supplies, routes, alliances, document access, and political trust

This fits the world because Solumora is about:

- constrained power
- route choice
- cross-kingdom tension
- institutional bottlenecks
- costly spell use
- consequences that accumulate

### Game Story Window

The best first game window is the same Kellvost-to-ceasefire crisis, but structured around interactive operations rather than full adaptation of every story beat.

Player-facing loop:

1. gather intelligence
2. choose who to trust
3. build mission loadout
4. execute tactical objective
5. absorb world-state consequences
6. stabilize or worsen corridor conditions

### Game Pillars

#### 1. Tactical Flux Use

- spells have Watt cost and discipline constraints
- Control Tier affects ceiling, reliability, and exhaustion
- rifts alter battlefield rules
- Hypertext appears late as high-risk exception technology

#### 2. Logistics as Gameplay

- route viability
- water, medicine, and transport limits
- who gets scarce support
- mission quality affected by preparation rather than only combat stats

#### 3. Institutional Negotiation

- Council access, guild access, archive access, expedition access
- decisions shift future support and information flow
- trust is a mechanic, not just dialogue flavor

#### 4. Mystery and Revelation

- incomplete ruins data
- conflicting interpretations
- Verath concealment
- hidden causality behind the cascade threat

### Best Game Playable Character Model

Use a fixed cast with partial viewpoint rotation, not a blank-slate protagonist.

Recommended playable or command-focus roles:

- Merra for field traversal and rescue operations
- Jess for strategic planning and logistics modifiers
- Kess for research, ruins interpretation, and anomaly diagnosis
- one flexible operations slot for a combat-capable field specialist

Verath should not be fully playable in the early game. Verath works best as a revelation and system-breaking late-game force.

### Vertical Slice Scope

The first playable vertical slice should cover:

- one Ashford investigation mission
- one Zakros route-planning and rescue mission
- one rift containment tactical mission
- one council or archive confrontation sequence

If that slice feels compelling, the full game is viable.

## Animation Alternative Path

If a full anime-style production is premature, produce a short-form animation package first.

Recommended package:

1. one 22-30 minute pilot focused on Kellvost aftermath and coalition formation
2. one 8-12 minute Zakros rescue short centered on Merra
3. one 8-12 minute logistics short centered on Jess
4. one 8-12 minute ruins-and-cascade short centered on Kess

This package proves:

- visual identity
- Flux language
- dramatic tone
- audience response to nonstandard protagonists
- viability of institutional thriller storytelling in animated form

## Franchise Strategy

### Recommended Release Order

1. Canon compression and adaptation bible
2. Animation proof package
3. Game vertical slice
4. Full animated season greenlight
5. Full game production
6. Anthology side stories after flagship tone is established

### Why This Order

- animation establishes the visual language of Flux, rifts, and kingdoms
- the game benefits from already-proven cast, tone, and iconography
- both projects can share concept art, environment bibles, spell language, and timeline compression work

## Required Preproduction Work

Before either project enters serious production, build these internal documents:

1. Adaptation Bible
   Compress vault canon into a 40-80 page production bible covering world summary, timeline, core cast, factions, magic rules, and the Kellvost-to-revelation arc.

2. Character Priority List
   Separate characters into:
   - flagship cast
   - recurring support cast
   - world texture cast

3. Timeline Lock
   Freeze the version of the crisis arc being adapted so later worldbuilding expansion does not constantly move the target.

4. Visual Development Pack
   Define kingdom shapes, district silhouettes, desert travel kit language, rift signatures, demon visual rules, and spell interface conventions.

5. Mechanics Translation Pack
   Convert Flux, Watts, Control Tier, sigils, Hypertext, rifts, and logistics pressure into game systems with clear boundaries.

6. Story Map
   Build a beat chart for the adaptation arc from Kellvost through ceasefire.

## Team Composition Recommendation

Minimum serious adaptation team:

- one canon lead
- one adaptation writer or narrative director
- one gameplay systems designer
- one art director
- one producer who can prevent scope explosion
- one continuity editor maintaining adaptation lock against vault drift

If separate teams are used for animation and game, the canon lead and continuity editor should be shared.

## What Not To Do

- do not begin with the entire deep-history chronology
- do not begin with a lore-explainer anthology without a main cast spine
- do not over-center combat at the expense of logistics, institutions, and risk
- do not make High Demons ordinary action villains
- do not build more than one flagship timeline before the first one is proven

## Milestone 1 Deliverables Completed

### One-Page Flagship Arc Summary

Kellvost is the ignition event: a destabilizing incursion that exposes a wider systems failure nobody can solve in isolation. The immediate aftermath drives accusation, denial, and fragmented emergency responses across institutions that were already strained.

Merra Veld, Jess Maren, and Kess Tarren each discover different fragments of the same pattern: the crisis is not a local anomaly but a cascade condition tied to brittle logistics, incomplete knowledge, and suppressed risk signals. Their parallel efforts reveal the same conclusion from different operational vantage points, creating credibility under pressure.

As institutions stall, Verath enters indirectly and then decisively as the hidden layer behind the public crisis map. The High Demon revelation shifts the conflict from conventional blame politics to existential governance and legitimacy shock.

The coalition phase forces former rivals into narrow-band cooperation. Ashford pressure, border hardening, and closed-door failures create the political and tactical runway for a last-resort joint stabilization operation.

The climax is a joint anchor operation under standstill conditions, where tactical success and institutional alignment are equally required. The resolution is not total peace; it is a ceasefire framework and a transformed world baseline that opens room for later arcs while preserving hard consequences.

### Release 1 Scope Lock

In-scope for first flagship timeline:

- Kellvost incursion and immediate aftermath
- border mobilization and institutional strain escalation
- Merra, Jess, Kess investigation convergence
- Verath reveal and Fifth Bell threshold material
- coalition formation and joint stabilization operation
- ceasefire framework and changed political baseline epilogue

Out-of-scope for first flagship timeline:

- full deep-history chronology as primary narrative path
- broad anthology-only structure without central cast spine
- post-ceasefire side-arc expansion as primary runtime focus
- full Wolfpoint-centric standalone arc beyond direct crisis relevance
- ancient civilization deep-dive beyond what is needed to explain cascade risk

### Canon Authority Assignment

- Canon lead: final authority on lore consistency and contradiction resolution
- Adaptation lead: final authority on scene-level adaptation choices within canon bounds
- Continuity editor: required sign-off gate for timeline, naming, and causality consistency
- Producer: final authority on scope cuts when continuity is preserved

Escalation rule:

- if adaptation and canon priorities conflict, continuity editor prepares decision brief, canon lead decides lore-safe options, producer selects shippable option

## Milestone 2 Deliverables Completed

### Adaptation Bible Foundation (Condensed)

World summary:

- Solumora is a politically fragmented, institution-heavy fantasy world where geography and route viability shape power.
- Zakros is a lethal equatorial barrier that enforces logistical scarcity and civilizational divergence.
- Terravelle, Auralis, and frontier corridors are operationally interdependent despite ideological conflict.

Magic and risk summary:

- Flux is universal but capacity-limited; power expression is constrained by Control Tier and practical cost.
- Watts, sigils, Hypertext, and hand-sign systems provide a rule language that supports readable consequences.
- Rift events and anomaly conditions change local operating rules and require coordinated responses.

Institution and faction summary:

- state, civic, archive, expedition, and logistics institutions are primary engines of both survival and failure.
- political conflict is amplified by information asymmetry, trust erosion, and constrained resources.
- High Demon concealment and revelation reframe legitimacy, law, and military doctrine.

### Character Priority Tiers

Flagship cast:

- Merra Veld
- Jess Maren
- Kess Tarren
- Teren Voss / Verath

Recurring support cast:

- Cassia
- Drest
- Eddan Voss

World texture cast:

- operators, clerks, couriers, expedition staff, tribunal and council staff, and route-level logistics roles supporting systemic realism

### Continuity Constraints and Forbidden Contradictions

Continuity constraints:

- Flux limits and Control Tier consequences must remain mechanically legible
- logistical pressure must remain story-relevant in all crisis operations
- institutions must act with internally consistent incentives even when flawed
- the revelation sequence must preserve political shock and downstream consequences

Forbidden contradictions:

- no cost-free high-output magic during core crisis beats
- no retroactive removal of institutional constraints to simplify plot resolution
- no early normalization of High Demons as ordinary adversaries
- no timeline compression that erases causality between Kellvost, reveal, and ceasefire

## Milestone 3 Deliverables Completed

### Franchise Pillars

- constrained power over spectacle dominance
- institutions under pressure over lone-hero simplification
- logistics and route viability as strategic drama
- mystery through partial truth and conflicting interpretation
- world-state consequence persistence after each major decision

### Guardrails (Do Not Do)

- do not open with deep chronology as mainline narrative
- do not over-center combat at the expense of logistics and governance
- do not flatten political actors into pure good-versus-evil simplifications
- do not split focus across multiple flagship timelines before first proof
- do not use exposition dumps where dramatized operations can teach rules

### Pillar Translation Map

Animation expression:

- constrained power: visible spell strain and failure cost in key scenes
- institutional pressure: cabinet, tribunal, and field-command conflict scenes with material consequences
- logistics drama: route denial, convoy risk, and supply prioritization as recurring stakes
- partial truth: staggered evidence reveal through parallel protagonist tracks
- persistent consequences: post-operation fallout visible in subsequent episodes

Game expression:

- constrained power: action economy and exhaustion linked to Flux usage
- institutional pressure: access gates tied to trust, permissions, and political standing
- logistics drama: mission readiness and route outcomes tied to preparation choices
- partial truth: intel uncertainty and branching interpretations before commitment
- persistent consequences: corridor status, support availability, and event states updated after missions

## Milestone 5 Ready-to-Run Validation Framework

Use this framework when animation proof and game vertical slice are available.

### Validation Rubric

Score each category as PASS, BORDERLINE, or FAIL.

- Rule clarity: audience can explain Flux cost and risk after first exposure
- Stakes readability: audience can identify what failure changes in the world-state
- Character anchoring: audience can distinguish Merra, Jess, and Kess operational roles
- Distinctiveness: audience identifies Solumora as institution-and-logistics-driven, not generic fantasy action
- Emotional continuity: participants can track tension escalation from incident to threshold

Gate logic:

- proceed if all categories are PASS, or if one category is BORDERLINE with an approved correction plan
- hold if any category is FAIL

### Feedback Capture Schema

Required capture fields:

- participant profile (animation-heavy, game-heavy, mixed, or new-to-genre)
- confusion points (rules, politics, cast, geography)
- strongest sequence
- weakest sequence
- one sentence summary of world identity after session
- recommendation: proceed, revise, or re-scope

### Milestone 5 Exit Package

- validation summary table with category outcomes
- top five corrections ranked by impact
- go/no-go recommendation with rationale

## Milestone 6 Scope Lock Decision Matrix

Apply this matrix after Milestone 5 validation.

Decision categories:

- core identity critical: keep regardless of production cost unless impossible
- high-value optional: keep if capacity allows without jeopardizing ship quality
- defer candidate: preserve in backlog for post-flagship expansion

Evaluation axes:

- identity impact
- continuity risk if removed
- production effort
- dependency complexity
- player or viewer comprehension value

Scope lock rule:

- any item with low identity impact and high production effort defaults to defer
- any item with high continuity risk requires continuity editor sign-off before removal

Milestone 6 exit requirements:

- locked must-have list
- explicit defer list with reasoning
- owner assignment for every in-scope deliverable

## Final Recommendation

Solumora is best positioned as a serious fantasy-political franchise built on constrained magic, institutional pressure, and world-state transformation.

The first adaptation should not try to summarize the world. It should prove that this world can deliver a focused, character-led crisis story with distinctive rules and consequences.

That means:

- flagship animation centered on Kellvost through revelation
- flagship game centered on tactical operations plus logistics during the same crisis window
- shared canon and visual bible supporting both

If this plan is followed, the world can grow outward into side stories, later coexistence arcs, Wolfpoint-focused projects, ancient-civilization material, and anthology expansions without losing clarity.
